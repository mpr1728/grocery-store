# 06. Launch plan

## Phases

| Phase | Length | What happens | Proof needed to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Validate | 4 to 6 weeks | No app. Pre-orders through WhatsApp and a simple page in 3 to 5 gated communities. Twice-weekly produce boxes, daily milk, fresh batters and two or three kitchen items, made in a rented licensed kitchen. | 150 or more paying households, repeat rate above 50 percent in week four, customers paying the planned price |
| 1. First hub | 3 months | Lease and fit out one hub. Launch the app with 800 to 1,200 items, on-demand and slot delivery, the morning run, and a small kitchen menu. Rule-based refill. | 300 or more orders a day, fill rate above 97 percent, fresh waste under control |
| 2. Make it smart | 3 months | Forecast models replace rules where they beat them. Household refill card. Private label for the first 20 staples. Meal to cart. | Forecast beats the baseline, contribution per order positive |
| 3. Second hub | After phase 2 | Repeat in an adjacent area so riders, suppliers, and the kitchen can be shared. | Second hub reaches break-even faster than the first |

Phase 0 matters most. It tests willingness to pay and the kitchen idea for the cost of a few weeks of effort, before any lease is signed.

## Unit economics (illustrative)

These are my planning assumptions to show how the model works. None of them are sourced figures. Replace each with a real quote or pilot result.

Per order:

| Line | Assumption | Rupees |
|---|---|---|
| Average order value | Premium basket with fresh and ready-made | 800 |
| Gross margin | 25 percent blended (staples low, private label and kitchen high) | 200 |
| Delivery | Rider cost per order | 45 |
| Picking and packing labor | | 15 |
| Packaging | | 10 |
| Payment fees | 1.5 percent | 12 |
| Waste and shrink | 2 percent of sales | 16 |
| **Contribution per order** | | **102** |

Hub fixed costs per month:

| Line | Assumption | Rupees |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | 3,500 sq ft at about 90 per sq ft | 3,20,000 |
| Staff not counted per order (manager, leads, kitchen, receiving, support) | | 4,50,000 |
| Power, cold chain, maintenance | | 1,20,000 |
| Technology, licences, other | | 1,10,000 |
| **Total** | | **10,00,000** |

Break-even: 10,00,000 divided by 102 is about 9,800 orders a month, or about 330 orders a day for one hub.

What moves the answer most:

- **Order value.** At 1,000 rupees with the same margin and costs, contribution rises to about 145 per order and break-even falls to about 230 orders a day.
- **Kitchen and private label share.** These carry the highest margins and lift the blended figure.
- **Morning run share.** Batched delivery costs far less per order than on-demand.
- **Waste.** One bad week in fresh can erase a month of contribution.

Not included: one-time fit-out and equipment (cold rooms, racking, kitchen, vehicles), marketing to acquire customers, and head office costs. Get real quotes for the fit-out before choosing the hub size.

## Compliance checklist

A list of what to confirm with a chartered accountant and a lawyer in the chosen state. I have not verified current thresholds or fees.

| Area | What to check |
|---|---|
| Entity | Private limited company or LLP, and who the shareholders are |
| Foreign ownership | India restricts foreign investment in inventory-based e-commerce and in multi-brand retail. If any founder or investor is a foreign citizen, or an Indian citizen resident abroad, the structure and whether the investment is treated as domestic need professional advice before incorporation |
| Food safety | FSSAI licence for each premises, with the right category for storage, retail, and the kitchen as a food production unit |
| Organic claims | Use of the word "organic" needs recognized certification and the Jaivik Bharat mark. "Residue-tested" and "natural" are separate claims with their own rules |
| Tax | GST registration, correct tax codes for food categories and prepared food |
| Packaged goods | Legal Metrology registration and label rules for private label packs |
| Local | Shops and establishments registration, trade licence, fire safety clearance, signage, pollution consent if the kitchen requires it |
| Meat and fish | Separate licence conditions and local rules |
| E-commerce and consumer | Consumer protection e-commerce rules, dark pattern guidelines, clear refund policy |
| Data | Digital Personal Data Protection Act: consent, purpose limits, children's data, breach handling |
| Labor | Contracts, statutory benefits for staff, terms for riders |
| Payments | Gateway onboarding and any cash handling rules |

## Risks

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Funded quick-commerce players copy the fresh and ready-made idea | Compete on trust and local kitchen quality, which are slow to copy, not on speed or discounts |
| Customers say they want clean food but buy on price | Phase 0 tests real payment before any lease |
| Fresh waste | Small frequent orders from suppliers, the kitchen as the outlet for surplus, end-of-day markdowns, daily waste review |
| A quality incident breaks the trust promise | Testing program, batch traceability so a recall is precise, open communication |
| Supplier inconsistency | Two sources for every key item, scorecards, receiving checks |
| Founder not on site | A strong local operating partner or hub manager with ownership is a requirement, not an option, if the founder is abroad |
| Too much technology too early | Phase 0 with no app, rules before models, few services |
| Rider availability in rain and festivals | Own core team, partner overflow, forecast-based rosters, honest longer promises in bad weather |
